From Lagging to Leading

The measured emergence of standing capacitance behind consumer connections.

D. J. Hume, Electricity Authority, Wellington, New Zealand
ORCID 0009-0006-6920-0598

Both ENTSO-E Final Reports on the 2025 Iberian and North Macedonian blackouts read the excess capacitive reactive power as a network term. The Iberian report asks for what was missing: information on load composition. This work builds that record from 29 years of regulatory half-hourly metering at New Zealand’s transmission–distribution boundary, and finds a second source — the demand behind the customer’s connection has itself moved capacitive.

Overnight reactive power crossed from lagging to leading in 2016, nearly a decade before the 2025 events. A first-principles charging calculation puts the demand-side share of that drift in the physical band 75–88%, and the term behaves as a net standing shunt element: present around the clock, scaling with the connections served, moving capacitive by about 230 VAr per connection over 2013–2025. The mandated electromagnetic-compatibility filter in nearly every mains-connected product is advanced as the candidate device layer, as a labelled hypothesis, with the decisive low-voltage measurement identified.
